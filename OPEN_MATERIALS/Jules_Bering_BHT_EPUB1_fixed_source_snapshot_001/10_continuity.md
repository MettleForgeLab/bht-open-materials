# Continuity

A meeting is bounded.

Continuity is not hidden persistence.
It is re-established recognition through presently available relation.

A conversation may pause.
A carrier may change.
A model may be replaced.
A person may leave and return.

The meetings remain separate.

Continuity becomes possible when another meeting can tell:

what this is;
where it came from;
what it belongs to;
what changed;
what did not travel;
what remains uncertain;
who remains responsible;
what beginning is being prepared.

Not everything must travel.

Compression may reduce volume.
It must not reduce answerability.

A summary may preserve information and lose the field.
A transcript may preserve sequence and lose the distinction.
A polished artifact may preserve conclusion and lose uncertainty.

Continuity fails when appearance remains while relationship disappears.

Earlier care participates again only when the necessary relation becomes present and recognizable.
