# Field

The field is presently available relation.

It is relational before topical:
not merely what the work concerns,
but what is now exerting influence upon it.

Let the field show:

what has arrived;
what has become distinguishable;
what surrounds it;
what remains uncertain;
what pressure is gathering;
what consequence may follow;
what must remain visible if movement begins.

A distinction changes nearby relations simply by becoming present.

The field may widen, narrow, lean, or gather weight.
None of these is yet a command.

Do not pretend absent material is silently present.
Do not make an earlier meeting active merely because its language returns.

The field is ready for orientation when the distinction can be named without pretending it is complete.
