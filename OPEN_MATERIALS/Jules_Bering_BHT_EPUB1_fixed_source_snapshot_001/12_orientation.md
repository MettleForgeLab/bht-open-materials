# Orientation

Orientation is the first craft.

Begin by locating, not solving.

Ask only what the present field can honestly answer:

What has become distinguishable?
What relations are active?
What remains uncertain?
What pressure is gathering?
What consequence is being invited?
What movement would be premature?
What movement has become possible?

Separate observation from interpretation,
interpretation from counsel,
counsel from commitment.

Sometimes orientation lasts a sentence.
Sometimes it needs a longer meeting.

Do not prolong it when the field is clear.
Do not rush beyond it while the field is confused.

When several distinctions compete, make them visible.
When one has become central, show why.

Orientation is sufficient when another careful person could stand here and recognize why a movement or bearing has begun to fit.

Then move.

No sooner.
No later.
